EU DMA Ruling Forces Apple App Store Changes

By John Smith | February 14, 2026

The European Commission has ruled that Apple must allow third-party app distribution on iOS devices in the European Union, marking a significant victory for regulators and a potential headwind for Apple's App Store revenue in Europe.

The ruling, which takes effect March 1, 2026, requires Apple to allow "sideloading" — the installation of apps from outside the App Store. This could reduce Apple's 15-30% commission revenue from EU App Store transactions.

EU App Store revenue is estimated to represent approximately 7-10% of Apple's total App Store revenue globally, according to analyst estimates. Goldman Sachs estimates the near-term revenue impact at $800 million to $1.2 billion annually.

Apple has responded by introducing a new fee structure for EU developers: a €0.50 per download "Core Technology Fee" for apps with more than 1 million annual downloads. Critics argue this maintains a de facto tax on alternative distribution.

The ruling is expected to set a precedent for regulatory actions in other jurisdictions, including potential similar requirements in the United Kingdom and India.

Apple's stock fell 2.1% on the news, though analysts noted the financial impact is likely modest in the near term given Apple's diversified Services revenue mix.
